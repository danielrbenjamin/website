Shop Inventory System

A React + Three.js inventory app for the team shop: an interactive 3D bay map, barcode scanning, respirator tracking, and crate manifests — built to kill the spreadsheet that nobody kept up to date.

Problem

A student engineering team's shop accumulates a huge amount of material, hardware, and safety equipment across dozens of storage bays — and the default way that gets tracked is a spreadsheet that's accurate for about a week after someone updates it. Nobody finds out inventory is wrong until they're standing in front of an empty bin looking for a part they were sure was there.

The Shop Inventory System exists to replace that spreadsheet with something that's actually easier to keep current than to ignore.

3D Bay Map

The centerpiece is an interactive 3D map of the shop's storage bays, rendered in Three.js inside a React app — instead of hunting through a spreadsheet row by row, a user can navigate the shop spatially, the way they'd actually walk through it, and see what's stored where.

Barcode Scanning & Tracking

Bins and crates are tracked via barcode scanning, which keeps updating inventory fast enough that people actually do it — scan in, scan out, done — rather than opening a spreadsheet and hunting for the right row. The system also tracks respirator issuance and condition, and maintains crate manifests for shipped or stored equipment, so safety-equipment tracking and shipping records live in the same tool as everyday parts inventory instead of scattered across separate ad-hoc systems.

Results

The system replaced a spreadsheet that was perpetually out of date with a tool people actually keep current, because updating it is faster than not updating it. That's the real bar for internal tooling on a volunteer student team — it only works if using it is easier than ignoring it.
