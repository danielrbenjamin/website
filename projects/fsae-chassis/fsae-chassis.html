Problem

UBC Formula Electric designs, builds, and races a formula-style electric car against other university teams every year. The chassis is the one system every other subteam bolts to — suspension, powertrain, aero, the driver — so it has to be right before almost anything else can be finalized. As Chassis Lead for the 2026 car, I own that structure from concept sketch through competition scrutineering.

The brief is really three competing constraints at once: keep the frame light enough to help lap time, stiff enough that suspension geometry doesn't wander under load, and strong enough to pass FSAE's structural rules and protect the driver in an impact. None of those are optional, and trading between them is most of the job.

Structure & Load Path

The frame is a 4130 chromoly tube-steel space frame, sized member by member from the loads it actually sees — suspension pickups, harness attachment points, impact attenuator mounting, and the torsional loads that come from cornering. I model the full assembly in SolidWorks and reason about load path before committing to a tube diameter or wall thickness, since moving a node in CAD is a lot cheaper than re-notching a tube.

  • Suspension pickups — sized against both simply-supported and fixed-fixed boundary assumptions, since the real mounting condition sits between the two
  • Torsional stiffness — modeled following the Deakin et al. approach, so front and rear stiffness stay matched to the suspension's designed weight-transfer balance
  • Impact attenuator & harness mounts — sized against FSAE's regulatory loads, including the 30 kN harness-anchor requirement

Analysis & Documentation

Every structural decision is backed by hand calculations and ANSYS Static Structural runs, and every one of those is written up in a formal design document — CHS-F04 for the chassis, SUS-F01 for the suspension-mounting interface — plus a maintained design-decision log. That's not paperwork for its own sake: FSAE's design event asks teams to defend their choices, and "here's the calc, here's the FEA, here's why we picked this tube" is a much stronger answer than "it hasn't broken yet."

It also means the next chassis lead inherits a frame they can actually reason about, instead of a black box they're afraid to touch.

Manufacturing

The frame is TIG-welded from notched 4130 tube, jigged to hold the suspension hardpoints within competition tolerance through the welding process. Uprights are custom-machined rather than off-the-shelf, and the planetary gearboxes and adjustable pedal box were both designed in-house to fit inside a chassis envelope that was never going to accommodate a catalog part.

Results

The 2026 chassis carries the full structural package — frame, uprights, gearboxes, pedal box, and aero mounting — through design, FEA, and fabrication as one coherent system, with every major decision traceable back to a calc or a documented tradeoff. That traceability is the part I'd point to: it's what lets the team defend the car at competition, and what lets whoever leads chassis after me pick up exactly where the documentation left off.
