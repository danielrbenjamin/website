Problem

A formula car's onboard electronics live in one of the worst environments you could design for: constant vibration, thermal cycling from the powertrain, exposure to rain and track debris, and almost no spare volume in the chassis to fit them. Every enclosure has to protect its board without stealing space or weight the rest of the car needs.

Off-the-shelf enclosures generally solve one of those problems, not all of them at once — they're either sealed and bulky, or compact and unsealed. Custom-designed housings, sized and rated to the specific board and mounting location, were the only way to hit every constraint at the same time.

Design Requirements

  • IPX5 water ingress rating — sealed against low-pressure water jets from any direction, since enclosures sit exposed near wheels and splash zones
  • Vibration tolerance — mounting had to hold a rigid board fixture through sustained road-course vibration without fatiguing fasteners loose
  • Thermal clearance — enough internal volume and venting geometry to keep board temperatures in range next to a powertrain that runs hot
  • Chassis-envelope fit — every enclosure had to fit the specific mounting location it was assigned, not a generic one

Vehicle Controller Enclosure

I led the Vehicle Controller enclosure design end to end — from board dimensions and connector clearances through to the sealed housing and its mount. The VC is one of the car's most safety-critical boards, so the enclosure design went through the same design-review process as structural chassis parts: dimensioned drawings, a defined seal strategy, and a mounting scheme that survives vibration without transmitting it straight into the board.

Mounting Systems

Beyond the enclosures themselves, I designed the mounting hardware that ties each box to the chassis — brackets sized for the vibration environment at their specific location, rather than a single generic mount reused everywhere. That meant treating each enclosure's mount as its own small design problem: what's the local chassis geometry, what's the expected vibration mode, and what's the lightest bracket that survives it.

Results

The enclosures shipped on the car and held their seal and mounting integrity through the season — no water ingress failures and no board losses from vibration-related mounting fatigue. Each enclosure's design is also handed down as a template for other subteams, since the same IPX5-and-vibration problem shows up anywhere electronics live on the vehicle.
