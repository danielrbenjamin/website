MEMS Soft-Actuated Hand

A soft-robotic hand driven by pneumatic actuators, modeled with fully articulated fingers — proximal, middle, and distal phalanges on each digit, hinged and driven like the physical hardware.

Problem

Most rigid robotic grippers are precise but unforgiving — they need exact part geometry and fail badly on anything unexpected. A soft, pneumatically-actuated hand trades some of that precision for compliance: it can wrap around irregular objects and tolerate positioning error, which makes it a better fit for gripping tasks where the object isn't perfectly known ahead of time.

The goal here was to model that kind of hand properly in CAD — not as a simplified stand-in, but with each finger's real joint structure — so the mechanism could be reasoned about and iterated on before committing to physical fabrication.

Model & Kinematics

Each of the five digits is modeled with a full three-joint chain: proximal, middle, and distal phalanges, hinged at pins rather than simplified into a single rigid curl. That matters because a real soft-actuated finger doesn't bend uniformly — the knuckle joint typically leads the curl, with the middle and distal joints following at different rates as the pneumatic chamber pressurizes.

  • Knuckle joint — carries the whole finger, bends first and most
  • Middle joint — carries the middle and distal phalanges together
  • Distal joint — carries just the fingertip segment, bends last and least

Modeling it this way in SolidWorks meant the assembly could be driven exactly the way the physical joints move, rather than approximated.

Live Model

The model below is the actual SolidWorks assembly, exported and running live in the browser. It grips and releases in a continuous loop, each finger curling knuckle-first the way the physical joints are designed to move. Drag to orbit and inspect it from any angle.

Pneumatic Actuation

The physical hand is driven by pneumatic actuators rather than motors or tendons — pressurizing a chamber embedded along each finger's underside causes it to curl, and releasing pressure lets it return to its open, relaxed shape. That's the same soft-robotics principle behind most PneuNet-style grippers: no rigid drivetrain inside the finger itself, just a compliant structure that responds directly to air pressure.

Results

The finished CAD model captures the full articulated kinematics of a five-finger soft-actuated hand, validated by driving it through a complete grip cycle entirely in software before any physical actuation was involved. That's the same model rendered live above.
